“I’d like a hot drink.”

Nobody tells the HSR to look for a mug. It picks the red one and decides to hold it by the handle.

It drives to where it can reach, then lines up with its hand camera.

8 of 11 real trials worked. The last four ran fully autonomously, back to back.

Language knew which part. Vision didn’t.

Ask an open-vocabulary segmenter for “the handle” and you get back almost the whole object. On the knife, the “handle” mask sat on the blade.

So I find parts geometrically in the point cloud instead, and the robot refuses a part it can’t see clearly rather than guess.

Knife, asked for the handle3 instructions, all the same97%
Remote, asked for a part3 instructions86–88%

How much of the whole object the “part” mask covered. 100% means the whole object.

And one paragraph in the prompt stopped it handing people a knife blade-first.

Stack and credits

GPT-4o, LangSAM (Grounding DINO + SAM), geometric part decomposition of the point cloud, Contact-GraspNet, and ROS Noetic on the robot. Perception runs on a CUDA PC; everything that touches the robot runs on the HSR workstation.

MSc dissertation at Heriot-Watt University and the National Robotarium, supervised by Dr Mauro Dragone. Graded A. Builds on AffordGrasp (Tang et al., IROS 2025).

Getting a humanoid to walk

A 17-joint MuJoCo humanoid. My first policy learned what I called survival locomotion: arms flailing, torso at 45°, falling slowly. TQC on Humanoid-v5, where the contact cost actually works, turned it into a gait.
