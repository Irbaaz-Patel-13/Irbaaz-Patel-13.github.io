“I’d like a hot drink.”

That’s the whole instruction. Nobody tells the robot to look for a mug.

It picks the red mug, and decides to hold it by the handle.

GPT-4o reads the head-camera image in three steps: what the task needs, which object fits, and which part to grasp. It also flagged that the mug might be hot.

Then it has to actually get there.

0 of 25 grasps reachable, then 25 of 25

The HSR’s 5-DoF arm can’t reach most grasps from where it starts, so a planner picks where to park first.

It reaches, and lines up using the camera in its hand.

46.9 px off target, then 12.7 px

Visual servoing corrects the last few centimetres before the gripper closes.

It closes until it feels the mug, then lifts 12 cm.

Depth has to confirm the mug came with it before the trial counts.

8 of 11 real trials worked. The last four ran fully autonomously, back to back.

Their lift heights were within 0.4 mm of each other.

Language knew which part. Vision didn’t.

This was the main finding of my dissertation.

Ask an open-vocabulary segmenter for “the handle” and it gives you back most of the object. On the knife, the “handle” mask sat on the blade. So I find parts geometrically in the point cloud instead, and the robot refuses a part it can’t see clearly rather than guess.

MSc Robotics dissertation, Heriot-Watt University and the National Robotarium. Supervised by Dr Mauro Dragone. Builds on AffordGrasp (Tang et al., IROS 2025). Graded A.

How the pipeline works
  • Reason. GPT-4o returns the object, the part and task constraints as JSON: surfaces to keep clear, grip firmness, what happens after the grasp. A contract check re-asks once if the answer contradicts itself.
  • Ground. LangSAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) finds the whole object. The point cloud is then split into structural parts and matched against the one requested.
  • Grasp. Contact-GraspNet proposes grasps on that part. The constraints set closing force, clearance, close depth and lift height.
  • Execute. On ROS Noetic: collision-aware base placement, a forward-kinematics check, hand-camera visual servoing, a contact-stop close, and a lift that depth has to confirm.

Getting a humanoid to walk

My first policy learned what I started calling survival locomotion: arms flailing overhead, torso hunched at 45°, falling slowly. Switching to TQC, dropping observation normalisation and moving to Humanoid-v5, where contact cost actually works, turned it into a gait.

Peak reward 5,742, up from about 400
