Beach litter detection for cleaning drones

A detector for aerial beach imagery, fast enough for a drone, feeding a planner that orders the pickups into a route.

Context
B.Tech capstone, NMIMS, 2024–25
Team
With Chirag Devgade and Priyam Kuvadiya. Supervised by Prof. Sachin Sonawane.
Published
Proc. ICNGT 2025, Routledge (Taylor & Francis)
Stack
YOLOv8-L, PyTorch, ONNX, TensorRT, CUDA, OpenCV, CVAT

The pipeline

  1. Detect

    YOLOv8-L trained on drone images of beaches, annotated in CVAT with a single litter class and an 80/20 train and validation split.

  2. Accelerate

    The PyTorch model exported to ONNX, then built into a TensorRT engine for an NVIDIA GPU.

  3. Locate

    An OpenCV chain (grayscale, Canny edges, dilation and erosion, contours, an area filter) gives each litter item a centroid from its image moments.

  4. Plan

    A greedy nearest-neighbour planner orders the centroids into a route, exported as JSON waypoints with timing.

Acceleration almost doubled throughput with accuracy roughly unchanged, and training changes then lifted mAP50 from 0.589 to 0.714 at the same speed.

What I'd change now: drive the planner straight from the detector's boxes rather than a separate contour pass, georeference the waypoints so a flight controller can use them directly, repeat runs with fixed seeds, and plan multi-item collection runs rather than returning to base after each pickup.
