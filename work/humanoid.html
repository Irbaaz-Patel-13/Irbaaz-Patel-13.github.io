Humanoid locomotion with deep RL

Getting a simulated 17-joint humanoid to walk forward without falling, and finding out why the first policy only learned to fall slowly.

Context
Independent project, 2026
My role
Solo: training, debugging, gait analysis
Environment
MuJoCo Humanoid-v5, 376-dimensional observations
Stack
Gymnasium, Stable-Baselines3, sb3-contrib TQC

The first attempt fell slowly

PPO with observation normalisation, 5 million steps, about an hour on an RTX 4050.

It reached a reward of around 400 and fell within 60 to 100 steps. Watching the video showed why: the agent had learned survival, not walking. Arms flailing overhead, torso hunched forward at about 45 degrees, barely any forward progress.

Three fixes

  1. Fix the environment version

    In Humanoid-v4 the contact-cost term of the reward is always zero, which breaks the shaping meant to discourage excess ground contact. Humanoid-v5 fixes it.

  2. Change the algorithm

    TQC can reach strong locomotion in about 2 million steps, where PPO typically needs 20 to 50 million.

  3. Turn off normalisation

    VecNormalize helps PPO, but it can hurt off-policy methods like TQC and SAC. The working configuration uses none, with a larger 400 by 300 network.

Results

First attempt (PPO)Final model (TQC)
Peak rewardAbout 4005,742
Episode lengthAbout 100 stepsUp to 1,000 steps
PostureHunched, about 45°Mostly upright, about 15°
ArmsFlailing for balanceControlled at the sides

Peak performance came at around 1.4 million of the 2 million steps, with noticeable variance afterwards. The final checkpoint scored 1,389, so the best policy is the saved peak, not the last one.

Next I'd train longer toward the 10,000+ reward benchmark, add reward terms for a periodic gait and natural arm swing, or use motion-capture references for imitation.
