Deep RL for pick-and-place

Teaching a simulated Franka Panda arm to pick up a cube from a random spot and place it at a random target, using only a sparse reward.

Context
Independent project, 2026
My role
Solo: environment setup, training, evaluation
Environment
PandaPickAndPlace-v3 (panda-gym, PyBullet)
Stack
Stable-Baselines3, sb3-contrib TQC, HER

The problem

Pick-and-place is seven skills in a row, and failing any one fails the episode.

Reach the cube, open the gripper, position, close, lift, carry, release. The reward is sparse: −1 for every step until the cube sits at the target. Early on, a random policy almost never gets there, so there's almost nothing to learn from.

Why TQC with hindsight replay

  • PPO stalled. After 500K steps it was still under 5% success. Being on-policy, it can't use hindsight relabelling.
  • HER makes the sparse reward usable. It relabels a failed episode as if wherever the cube ended up had been the goal, so every episode carries signal.
  • TQC beat SAC here. Its distributional critics, with the most extreme quantiles truncated, reduce overestimation. In my runs it was roughly 20 to 30% more sample-efficient than SAC.
  • A shorter horizon mattered. Dropping gamma from the default 0.99 to 0.95 helped, because pick-and-place doesn't need to plan far ahead.

How training went

1M timesteps, about 27 hours of training.

  1. 0 to 200K steps

    Mostly exploration. Success hovered between 0 and 10% while the replay buffer filled.

  2. 200K to 700K steps

    Grasping emerged and episodes got shorter. Training success reached about 20 to 25%, with evaluation swinging between 5 and 40%.

  3. 700K to 850K steps

    The agent worked out lift-and-carry. Success jumped from about 30% to 70% in 150K steps.

  4. 850K to 1M steps

    Refinement. Success climbed past 95% and episode length fell from about 25 steps to about 10.

Results

MetricValue
Success rate100% (20 of 20 evaluation episodes)
Average episode length9.4 steps
Mean reward−7.35 ± 2.59
Training1,000,000 timesteps, about 27 hours

What didn't work: distance-based dense rewards made the agent hover near the cube without grasping it. Bigger networks, more parallel environments and more sampled goals per episode didn't help and slowed training down.

The main lesson: hyperparameters like gamma, learning rate and batch size moved results far more than network size, and watching episodes caught problems the metrics missed.
